Day 05 Wed · 11 Nov 2026 Queenstown · Arrowtown · Glenorchy

Gold-rush cottages,
then a road into Paradise.

A circular day from Hampshire Lakeview — sycamores and stonemasons in the cool of an Arrowtown morning, then the group of 6 splits into two threes for the 13:00 activity slot (TSS Earnslaw for one three, Skyline Luge for the other), reconvenes at Hampshire by 14:40, and drives together west along Lake Wakatipu’s edge to Glenorchy.

distance~140 km
diesel~21 L
fuel cost~NZ$65
13:00 splitEarnslaw + Luge

Why Arrowtown morning, Glenorchy afternoon?

The day is built around two natural rhythms: a quiet old town, and a long lakeshore drive. Both have a "right" time of day, and they sit on opposite sides of Queenstown — so the loop is clean if you sequence them correctly.

Light & crowds favour morning

Buckingham Street's sycamores and miners' cottages face east-southeast — soft, low light hits the verandahs before 10:30. The first Queenstown coach tours arrive 10:30–11:30 and motorhome parking on Ramshaw Lane tightens fast. Arrive 09:00, leave by 11:00.

Glenorchy Road is a westbound drive

From Queenstown, the road curves northwest along the lake's western shore. Going out in the afternoon puts the sun behind your right shoulder — every lookout (especially Bennetts Bluff) photographs cleanly, and the red Glenorchy boat shed glows against the Humboldts from 16:00 onward.

The 1pm split works because both venues are on the same street

Hampshire is on Brecon St. Steamer Wharf is 5 min downhill; Skyline Gondola is 2 min uphill. The group of 6 splits into two threes at 12:35, does parallel activities, and reunites at Hampshire by 14:40 — a 4-min drive from the fuel stop and the Glenorchy road. Neither group has to compromise; both fit inside the same afternoon.

PARK

Park the motorhome — Ramshaw Lane car park

Ramshaw Lane, behind Buckingham St · long-vehicle spaces along the back fence · Google Map ↗

The main Buckingham Street parking is for cars only. Ramshaw Lane is two minutes' walk from the heritage street and the only sensible motorhome park in town. Free, no time limit, public toilets on-site. Get here before 09:30 — by 10:30 the coach tours arrive and the long bays go first.

Heads-up Do NOT try to drive a 7m motorhome down Buckingham St itself. It's one-way, narrow, angled-parking on both sides. Park at Ramshaw and walk in.

WANDER

Buckingham Street — heritage stroll & coffee

Buckingham St, 1860s miners' cottages, 24 trees each side · Google Map ↗

Walk east down Buckingham toward the Arrow River. Sixty-plus original gold-rush buildings still standing, many now boutique shops, galleries, and cafés. The sycamores arching over the street are spectacular in spring green by mid-November. Don't miss the Mary Cotter sycamore outside the old Cotter cottage — there's a small plaque dedicated to a two-year-old in 1867.

Coffee + breakfast at Provisions of Arrowtown (34 Buckingham St) — set in a historic miner's cottage with a back garden. The Eggs Benedict and the pastries are both legendary; NZ$18–32 for a main. Or a quicker stop at The Chop Shop (Arrow Lane) — locals' favourite for flat whites.

Sweet stop for the group The Remarkable Sweet Shop and Patagonia Chocolates are both on Buckingham — pick up roadtrip snacks for the Glenorchy drive.

HISTORY

Arrowtown Chinese Settlement — free, riverside

Bush Creek end of Buckingham St · Google Map ↗

A two-minute walk past the western end of Buckingham, beside Bush Creek, you'll find a cluster of restored stone-and-tin huts. From 1869, Chinese gold-miners — invited by the government when European miners moved west to the Coast — were forced to live separately here. Ah Lum's Store is the standout: fully restored 1880s provisions shop with original counters and ledgers. Information panels along the path, free entry, allow 25–30 minutes.

SPLIT ACTIVITY

Two activities running in parallel — meet back at Hampshire 14:40

Steamer Wharf lakefront · & · Bob’s Peak elevated · Hampshire rendezvous · Rendezvous ↗
GROUP A · TSS EARNSLAW

13:00 sailing, dockside 14:30

1912 coal-fired steamship · seated · card-only bar

90-min cruise-only loop toward Walter Peak and back. Open decks fore & aft, indoor lounges if wet, engine room mezzanine (stokers shovelling coal in real time), Promenade Café & Bar with wine, beer, coffee, cabinet food.

Ten-minute rhythm: deck for departure shot, engine room for the brass-and-hot-oil moment, then settle at the Promenade with a drink for Walter Peak growing then receding. Return leg lights the Queenstown approach beautifully.

Different lake angle from Bennetts Bluff — Earnslaw goes west across the lake at water level; Bennetts is elevated north view. Not view-redundant.

GROUP B · SKYLINE LUGE

3 rides, back at base ~14:20

Bob’s Peak 480m up · gondola + chairlift + sled

Steepest cable car in the Southern Hemisphere — 10 min up. At the top: chairlift to luge start, three 15-min rides each including the ride back up. Try blue (mellow) then red (fast banked corners), then repeat the favourite.

Views — 360° over Wakatipu, Remarkables, Coronet, and the whole Queenstown basin. Better light than Bennetts Bluff will give later, but similar angle. The gondola-station observation deck is the Instagram shot.

Weather fallback: if the luge closes (red first, then blue in heavy rain/wind), stay for gondola + Stratosfare café at the top, meet Group A back at Hampshire at 14:40 anyway.

REUNITE · 14:40 at Hampshire Group B (Luge) typically arrives back ~15 min ahead of Group A (Earnslaw). Coffee at the Hampshire kitchen or pack the van for the Glenorchy drive while waiting. If Earnslaw is delayed docking, Group A texts Group B and the van waits — the fuel stop is only 4 min drive; departure can slip 15 min without hurting Bob’s Cove or Bennetts.
Verify before booking [medium confidence] TSS Earnslaw had scheduled annual maintenance mid-2026; confirm she’s back in service on 11 Nov via RealNZ. Skyline runs regardless of the Earnslaw status — the split can proceed with either combo. If Earnslaw is out, the Spirit of Queenstown catamaran runs the same route as a backup for Group A.
PART 03 · AFTERNOON

The drive to Paradise

FUEL

BP Queenstown Truckstop — high-flow diesel

131 Gorge Rd, Queenstown · 24/7 · truckstop pricing · Google Map ↗

Both groups back at Hampshire by 14:40 (Group B ~15 min ahead of Group A). Collect the van and drive 4 min to Gorge Rd. The only true truckstop in Queenstown — high-flow diesel nozzles, drive-through bays sized for tour buses, and prices typically 15–25¢/L cheaper than the BP Connect or Z on Frankton Rd. There's no fuel between Queenstown and Glenorchy, and you don't want to drive that road on a quarter tank. Top up to full (~40 L for the Maui River — see fuel panel below).

Why here, not on Frankton Road BP Connect Queenstown (1094 Frankton Rd) and Z Queenstown are both forecourt-only, standard nozzles, retail pricing — fine for cars, slow for a Maui's 95-litre tank. Gorge Rd Truckstop fills it in under 4 minutes.

DRIVE

Queenstown → first lookouts — Glenorchy-Queenstown Road

SH6A through town, then Glenorchy-Queenstown Rd · Route ↗

The road is fully sealed and motorhome-friendly the whole way to Glenorchy — but it's windy with steep drop-offs and sharp corners. Easy 80 km/h pace, no hurry. The first informal pull-out is at Little Thailand Viewpoint (4 km from town, gravel bay on the right) — quick 3-min stop for the first big lake view, or skip if you want to reach Bob's Cove sooner.

Both activities land you on the Glenorchy road by 15:00 Earnslaw returns 14:30, Skyline group typically back ~14:20 — both groups reconvene at Hampshire by 14:40, fuel by 15:00, on the Glenorchy road. That’s time for Bob’s Cove full 60-min loop, Bennetts Bluff in good afternoon light, and a full 75-min Glenorchy Wharf window with the walkway. The split doesn’t cost the afternoon anything.

SHORT WALK

Bob's Cove Track — forest, jetty, lookout

Glenorchy-Queenstown Rd, 17 km from Queenstown · large gravel car park · Google Map ↗

A 1.5 km loop through beech forest opens to a turquoise cove, then a short steep climb to a lookout knoll. Roughly 30 min walking + time at the jetty and lookout — the full hour means unhurried pace, photos at the cove, and the climb to the viewpoint without rushing. One of the best short walks in the whole Wakatipu basin; the sheltered cove looks tropical on a calm afternoon (lake's still glacier-cold; nobody's swimming in November). Toilets at the car park, easy motorhome parking.

LOOKOUT

Bennetts Bluff Lookout — the premier viewpoint

Glenorchy-Queenstown Rd, ~25 km from Queenstown · purpose-built 2021 lookout · Google Map ↗

This is THE photo of the Glenorchy drive. Two hundred metres past the old roadside pull-out, a proper purpose-built car park (large enough for the Maui), a 3-minute walk to the platform, picnic tables, toilets. Views open south over the Wakatipu narrows to Pigeon and Pig Islands, and north up the lake to the Humboldts and Mt Earnslaw. Late afternoon sun (around 16:40–17:05) lights the western flank of the mountains and turns the lake aluminium-blue.

Best photo spot Walk past the main platform another 30 m along the path for the view of the road itself snaking down to the lake — the "iconic" Glenorchy Road shot.

PHOTOS + WALK

Glenorchy Wharf, Red Shed & Walkway — golden hour

Mull St / Oban St · free public car park at the wharf · Google Map ↗

Park at the lakefront. The famous red boat shed sits on the jetty — once a freight depot for the TSS Earnslaw (Group A rode her 4 hours ago; she used to run all the way up here delivering supplies to remote farm stations before the road was built in 1962). Now the most photographed shed in the South Island. The Humboldt Mountains rise behind it; in afternoon light (~17:40) the peaks are still in sun while the shed sits in shadow — perfect for a silhouette shot.

Then the Glenorchy Walkway — with the 13:00 Earnslaw sailing you have the time for the full 1-hour boardwalk loop through the lagoon wetland behind town. Bird life (paradise ducks, scaup, occasional fernbird), 360° mountain views, almost no tourists. Mt Earnslaw glacier glows pink toward sunset. This is the moment you remember the trip for.

Lord of the Rings note If you're a fan: the flats north of Glenorchy (toward Paradise) appeared as Lothlórien, Isengard, and the muster of Rohan. Paradise itself is 20 km further on a partly-gravel road — too far for today, but the walkway crosses some of the same vista.

60 min
DINNER

Dinner at Mrs Woolly's General Store or Glenorchy Café

47 Oban St / Mull St · Mrs Woolly's ↗ · Glenorchy Café ↗

Two solid choices, both walking distance from the wharf. Mrs Woolly's — beautifully restored, woodfired pizzas (NZ$22–28), curated NZ wines and craft beer, fire roaring inside. Kitchen closes 19:00 sharp in shoulder season — arriving at 18:45 means you must be seated and ordering immediately. Call ahead (+64 3 442 9994) to warn them the group of 6 is coming from the walkway; some staff will hold a slot. Glenorchy Café — homier, more pies/burgers/lamb, open until 20:00 most nights. Safer default if you want to actually browse a menu.

The honest call If Mrs Woolly's is the goal, cut the Glenorchy Walkway short (20-min out-and-back instead of the full loop) and arrive Mrs Woolly's by 18:30. If you want the full walkway loop, eat at Glenorchy Café. You can't comfortably have both.

Distance breakdown: Hampshire → Arrowtown (22 km) → Queenstown Hampshire via SH6 (22 km) → Gorge Rd Truckstop (~3 km) → Glenorchy (47 km) → Hampshire (47 km). Total ~141 km, ~21 L for the day.

Fuel cost calc [medium confidence — verify day-of on Gaspy]. Queenstown diesel typically runs 20–30¢/L above the NZ national average due to the remote tourist location. National avg mid-2026 was NZ$2.43–2.69/L, so Queenstown retail forecourts sit around NZ$2.65–2.95/L. Truckstop pricing at BP Gorge Rd is typically 15–25¢/L cheaper — estimated NZ$2.50–2.65/L. Using midpoint NZ$2.55: 21 L × NZ$2.55 = NZ$53.55 for the diesel consumed today. If you top up to full tank at Gorge Rd (recommended before Glenorchy), you'll pump ~40 L → ~NZ$100.

Road User Charges (RUC). The Maui rental includes RUC — you pay this in the daily rental rate as a per-km recovery, not at the pump. Reference figure only: at NZ's standard diesel RUC of ~NZ$8 per 100 km for light diesel vehicles, today's 140 km costs the rental company (and indirectly you) ~NZ$11. Total real cost of moving the motorhome today: ~NZ$65 (fuel + RUC), or ~NZ$100 for the full cash outlay at the pump if topping up. Not per person — that's for the whole vehicle.
